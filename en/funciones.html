Your whole library in one place

Steam, Epic, GOG, Xbox, Ubisoft, EA, Battle.net, Amazon, Rockstar and games that have no launcher, together.

GameHub looks at what you already have on your PC: the folders and files each store's launcher keeps. That's how it finds your installed games and also the ones you own but haven't installed, with their covers and the hours you've played. Hours are counted even if you open the game from its own launcher.

In the sidebar, clicking Library opens your stores with how many games you have in each one (also the ones whose app is installed even if they have no games yet). You can sort by name, by last played, by hours or by size, search instantly and see what you played recently.

Games without a launcher (an emulator, an itch.io game…) are added with the “Add game” button, or you tell GameHub which folders hold your loose games and it finds them on its own. It looks for the official Steam cover also for games from other stores, added by hand or delisted from the store; if there's none, it uses the game's icon, and you can always set your own image.

A game you have in several stores or in the same folder shows up only once. And if Steam is updating a game, you see it on its card (“Updating 45%” or “Update pending”).

If something that isn't a game slips in (a program, a server…), you remove it and GameHub learns. You can get it back in Options → Library.

It never asks for the password of any store: to play, it opens each game with its own launcher, as if you'd opened it yourself.

How to use it

  • Open it and wait a few seconds: your games show up on their own.
  • Double-click or the “Play” button (below the cover) to open a game.
  • “Show N not installed” also shows the ones you haven't installed.
  • “Find games” checks your stores again right away.

Each game's page

Everything about a game in one panel: how it runs on your PC, your FPS, your settings and your saves.

Clicking a game opens its page, in three tabs: Overview (your hours, the last time you played, how much space it takes, the requirements, your FPS and your graphics settings), Saved games and Info (your achievements, the description, the store, the studio, the release date, where it's installed and where it saves).

Runs on my PC? GameHub knows your CPU, your graphics card and your memory, and compares them with each game's minimum and recommended requirements on the Steam store. You'll see a traffic light: green if it runs with room to spare, yellow if it meets the minimum and red if it falls short. In the library you can filter to see only the games that run with room to spare, the ones that run or the ones that don't.

And also how many FPS you'll get, with a range (“≈ 75 FPS (65-85)”) and at each quality: Low, Medium, High and Ultra. If you've already played it, your real FPS come first (only the ones from your current GPU and CPU); if not, it's worked out from the community's sessions (if you share yours or have ArlequinPro) or, as an approximation, from the Steam requirements.

What limits you and what's worth upgrading? GameHub works out separately what your graphics card would give and what your CPU can handle, and tells you clearly: “The GPU is the limit: a better CPU would barely raise your FPS”, or the other way round, or that they're evenly matched and you'd have to upgrade both.

It also has its saved games grouped (saves, settings…), its backups and buttons to open its folder or install it.

How to use it

  • Click any game in the library and switch tabs at the top.
  • The “On my PC” filter in the bar at the top of the library.

Change each game's graphics settings

Quality, resolution, display mode, FPS limit, VSync… from GameHub, without opening the game.

GameHub reads the graphics settings each game saves and shows them on its page. In games that save their settings in a known format, you can also change them: the main ones (overall quality, resolution, display mode, FPS limit and VSync) are right there, and “All settings” opens a window with all of them, grouped and with a search box.

It understands games made with Unreal Engine (Borderlands 3 and 4, Hogwarts Legacy, THE FINALS…), Unreal Engine 3 (Rocket League, Killing Floor 2…), Unity and Source (Counter-Strike 2…), plus GTA V (Legacy and Enhanced), Cyberpunk 2077 and Far Cry 2 to 6.

Before the first change it keeps a copy of how everything was: with “Restore original” you leave it as it was. With the game open it won't let you change anything (the game would overwrite it on exit) and it respects read-only files.

How to use it

  • On the game's page, “Your graphics settings”.
  • Change whatever you want and click “Save”: you'll notice it the next time you open the game.

Your real FPS

Measures FPS while you play, without touching the game, and tells you what limits you.

With PresentMon, a free and open-source tool from Intel, GameHub watches from outside when the game draws each frame: it doesn't inject anything, so it doesn't bother anti-cheats. When you close the game it keeps a summary: average FPS, median, 1% low (how smooth it runs) and stutters.

It notes your graphics settings for that session (quality, resolution, DLSS/FSR, frame generation, ray tracing, VSync, FPS limit) and tells you what limits your performance: the GPU, the CPU or an FPS cap (if you were capped by VSync or by a game or driver limit, you'll see “Capped” instead of blaming the CPU). Plus the RAM, graphics memory and virtual memory the game used, in case you're running short.

We've checked that it measures correctly: with an animation running at exactly 360 FPS, GameHub read 358.8. And if you want to see them while you play, put them on the HUD.

How to use it

  • Turn it on in the welcome screen or in Options → Your PC (it's optional). Windows asks for permission once and it starts measuring after you sign out or restart.
  • Play for a while and close the game: you'll see it on its page, under “Your FPS”.

Your PC at a glance

Your components, your internet and what you still need to turn on to get the most out of it.

GameHub shows your CPU, your graphics card, your memory (speed, latency and modules), your drives, your screens with their refresh rate, your keyboard and mouse, and your internet speed with a test that takes about 25 seconds.

And it warns you about what often gets left switched off: if your memory runs slower than it says on the box because the XMP profile (EXPO on AMD DDR5) is off in the BIOS, or if your PC supports Resizable BAR (Smart Access Memory on AMD) and it's disabled. Both give you free FPS in many games, and it tells you what they're called in each brand's BIOS.

How to use it

  • Options → Your PC.
  • “Test speed” for the internet test (the automatic one, once a month, isn't run on mobile data).

Play Hub: to play from the couch

A full-screen interface made for the controller and the TV.

With F11 or from the sidebar, GameHub turns into a big interface to use with a controller: huge covers, the selected game in the background with its info, and smooth movement, one at a time.

At the top you have tabs: “All” and one for each store. You switch tabs with L1/R1 (LB/RB on Xbox) and each one has its rows: Continue, Most played and your whole library. When you go back to a tab you're right where you left off.

It recognises Xbox, PlayStation and Switch controllers and changes the buttons you see to match the one you use (or the ones you choose). It also works with the keyboard (arrows, WASD, Q/E) and the mouse wheel. Games without a cover get one of their own, with their name and colours.

How to use it

  • F11 or “Play Hub” in the sidebar.
  • In Options → Controller and Play Hub you choose the button style.

Built-in chat

Discord, Steam Chat and TeamSpeak next to your games.

Opens each service's chat inside GameHub, in a panel you can resize to whatever width you like or pop out into its own window. Each service turns on and off separately, with its own sound, and with TeamSpeak you see who's talking in your channel.

On top of that, your Discord status shows “Arlequin GameHub” and the game you're playing, with how long you've been at it (you can turn it off in Options → When playing).

While you play, the chat can stay connected even when GameHub closes its window to leave everything to the game: you choose it in Options → When playing.

How to use it

  • Click “Chat” in the sidebar and pick the service.
  • Options → Chat to choose services and sounds.

Lucky box

Don't know what to play? Let luck decide.

A roulette in the style of in-game loot boxes that picks one of your games. You can choose between installed games or not, from which store, and whether it goes by hours played or by achievements: games you've never opened are the most likely to come up, and the ones you've played the most, the rarest.

You get 5 spins a day (unlimited with ArlequinPro).

How to use it

  • In the sidebar, “Lucky box”.
🥔

POTATO MODE

For PCs that are short on resources: GameHub in an ultra-light native window.

POTATO MODE restarts GameHub without its web interface (with no browser inside): just your games and a button to play. It uses far less memory and keeps backing up your saves and counting your hours.

POTATO MODE+ (ArlequinPro) goes even further: just the launcher, with nothing in the background, so your whole PC is for the game.

How to use it

  • The “POTATO MODE” button in the sidebar (and “+” for POTATO MODE+).
  • In Options → Performance you can make it always start like this and choose which one.
  • When you open a game, GameHub switches to POTATO MODE on its own (it closes its window and the chat to leave everything to the game) and comes back as it was when you close it. In Options → When playing you choose whether it closes the window and whether it disconnects the chat (for example, to stay in a Discord call).

Your way

Themes, language and how GameHub behaves.

Pick a theme (Arlequin, Midnight, pitch-black Dark or white-background Light; with ArlequinPro, also Sunset and Neon), in English or Spanish. For PCs with few resources there's light mode, which removes animations, transparency and glow (you can already turn it on in the welcome screen).

GameHub can start with Windows hidden in the system tray (to count your hours and back up your saves even if you play from another launcher). It updates itself (if you skip versions, it shows you all the news together) and the first time it shows you your games by store so you can remove anything that isn't a game.

How to use it

  • Options: each page explains at the top what you set up in it.
